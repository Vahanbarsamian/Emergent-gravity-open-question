## Abstract

We propose an emergent scalar field model of gravity where the gravitational potential $\Phi(r)$ arises from local deformations of a phase coherence field $C(\mathbf{x})$. By enforcing an absolute upper bound $C_{\text{max}} = 1.0$ and a critical vacuum coherence baseline $C_c = 0.2000$, the model naturally resolves gravitational infinities at small spatial scales while recovering standard Newtonian and Einsteinian behaviors in the weak and strong field limits. Applied to observational astrophysical data, the model correctly accounts for the flat rotation curves of spiral galaxies (tested on Andromeda M31) without dark matter halos, matches gravitational redshift observations (S2 star near Sgr A*), and predicts a shadow diameter for supermassive black holes (M87*) consistent with Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) measurements while eliminating the central singularity ($r \to 0$).

## 1. Mathematical Formulation

We define the local phase coherence $C(\mathbf{x}) \in [0, 1]$. In vacuum, $C(\mathbf{x}) = C_c = 0.2000$. Matter sources distort the coherence field, generating a coherence deficit/excess $\Delta C(\mathbf{x}) = C(\mathbf{x}) - C_c$.

The emergent gravitational potential $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$ is governed by the modified Poisson-like equation:

$$\nabla^2 \Phi(\mathbf{x}) = 4\pi G \alpha_{\text{emergence}} \left( C(\mathbf{x}) - C_c \right)$$

where $\alpha_{\text{emergence}} \approx \frac{c^2}{G}$ sets the coupling scale between coherence field perturbation and effective stress-energy density.

### Singularity Avoidance Mechanism
In classical General Relativity, $r \to 0$ leads to a curvature singularity ($\rho \to \infty$). In this framework, as $r \to 0$, $C(r)$ asymptotically approaches $C_{\text{max}} = 1.0000$. The saturation of the coherence field caps the spatial gradient $\nabla C(r)$, causing the effective local acceleration $g(r) = -\nabla \Phi(r)$ to vanish at the absolute center ($g \to 0$), substituting the central point singularity with a saturated phase core.
